2. Choose between acting and reviewing

Assume correct automatic actions cost zero and review is perfectly accurate. Choose the action with the lowest expected cost; the probability alone does not determine the best action.

Try this

At p = 0.5 and the default costs, review is cheapest. Raise review cost to 0.8: review has no strict advantage at any probability. Raise p to 0.99 with the default costs: acting yes is cheaper than review.

These policies depend on calibrated probabilities, correct costs, and the stated review assumption. Real reviewers make mistakes. Permissions remain separate deterministic checks.
